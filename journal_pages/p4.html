Notes, by: Skyla Trousdale

Journal P4

Reading: What is code? by Paul Ford

Terms

Term: definition

"Can you tell me what code is?" p5

"Here is what you’ve been told: All of the computer code that keeps the website running must be replaced." p3

I think the variable nature of software is very interesting; with a technology booming with innovation and old tech having a quick turnover rate, its hard to adapt to the changes as they happen. If we lose the perpetual need to update dependancies. The emphasis on human inability to estimate computer programming timelines, sometging I can vouch for in my own experience, further begs the question in my mind - can AI best us at time estimates and updating dependancies...?

"[Software engineer] gives you a number and a date. You know in your soul that the number is half of what it should be and that the project will go a year over schedule" p4

Neighborhoods of Code

There are lots of other neighborhoods, too: There are people who write code for embedded computers smaller than your thumb. There are people who write the code that runs your TV. There are programmers for everything. They have different cultures, different tribal folklores, that they use to organize their working life. p6

In this writing, the author rerers to different platforms and systems as different neighborhoods of programmers; related, but ultimately different fields.

"Every month, code changes the world in some interesting, wonderful, or disturbing way." p6-7

Everything is becoming computers, and Ford emphasizes that computers are taking differnt forms, from small personal devices to entire systems -- anything can be a computer. Inspiring, but carying the implication frome earlier of responsibility in upkeep, keeping dependancies in order with the rollout of new technology, and the difficulty and cost of implementing these updates.

"So many things are computers, or will be. That includes watches, cameras, air conditioners, cash registers, toilets, toys, airplanes, and movie projectors. Samsung makes computers that look like TVs, and Tesla makes computers with wheels and engines. Some things that aren’t yet computers—dental floss, flashlights—will fall eventually." p8
