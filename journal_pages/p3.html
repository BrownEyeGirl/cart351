Limits of Human Perception

Our perception is limited. As data becomes measured more and the internet becomes largely a place where this unintelligable by the human mind alone data is stored, we give up our ability to be primary interpreters of data. I think this is so interesting because the internet has always been a place adapted to us with UI and UX, and algorithms to interpret and organize the noise. As this space grows and everything become digitalized, these algorithmic intermediaries become translators for us.

"Contemporary perception is machinic to large degrees. The spectrum of human vision only covers a tiny part of it"

Drowning in Data

“We are drowning in material." - WikiLeaks Julian Assange

This reminds me of the epstein files leak. When mass amounts of data are available, we in turn get overwhelmed. it becomes difficult for humans to decide what has value and how we should be reacting and valuing the data. It is suffocating, it can even feel like drowning...

Perfect Binary Data and Human Error

Essential services rely on human input of data because, end of the day, the data is human. Just as the machines translate data and present it to us, we translate our data into machine language; However, machines cannot understand nuances and the objective binary decision making order of the machine leaving us as filling out data as either "correct" or "incorrect". No in betweens. This is interesting to me, perhaps this issue is solvable through highly invasive data access / scraping the internet. This idea terrifies me, so I would imagine the error is like our tithe to the machine for the machine to pretend it does not hold all the data about us anyways.

"Everyone is lying all the time, whenever possible, or at least cutting corners. Not surprisingly, the “dirtiest” area of data collection is consistently pointed out to be the health sector, especially in the US. Doctors and nurses are singled out for filling out forms incorrectly." - Hito Steyerl

Industrialized Data & Its Consequences

"All sorts of agricultural and mining technologies—that were developed during the neolithic— are reinvented to apply to data. The stones and ores of the past are replaced by silicone and rare earth minerals, while a Minecraft paradigm of extraction describes the processing of minerals into elements of information architecture" - Hito Steyerl

The vocabulary deployed for separating signal and noise is surprisingly pastoral: data “farming” and “harvesting,” “mining” and “extraction” are embraced as if we lived through another massive neolithic revolution with it’s own kind of magic formulas." - Hito Steyerl

Something that stands out to me in these quotes are their link to labour reform. In the industrual revolution, machines automated repetative tasks and were so powerful they completely reshaped how labour was performed. From simple calculated tasks completed from beginnign to end, suddenly machines could specialize in particular areas of farming and production, leaving the gaps for humans to fill. This resulted in repetative tasks as a new form of labour. With machines being responsible for interpreting data, mining it, extracting it, and synthesizing it, it replaces the human need to read, analyze basic data sets, and filter data because of the mass amount of it available. I find this very interesting, as the world we know is increasingly written in overwhelming raw data and code, we may consequently fill in the gaps left by machines. In mining technology, humans supervise the machines and control their placement, detonation, and power/processing. We are facilitators to the machine. In a world where data is mined, farmed, extracted, we dont just become the facilitators of the machines, but the crop, the minerals themselves that the machines manage...
