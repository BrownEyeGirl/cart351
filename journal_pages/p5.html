Travels in Intermedia[lity], an introduction

Notes, by: Skyla Trousdale

Journal P5

Reading: travels in intermedia[lity] by Mark Bernstein

Terms

intertextuality: the shaping of texts meaning by another text; no work stands alone it exists in the context and references of others.
intermediality: the overlap/intersection of different communication mediums, such as text, image, sound, digital platforms etc
heterogeneous: diverse in character or content
hegemony: leadership or dominance, esp by one social group over the other.
logocentricism: view of words or language as a central fundamental expression of external reality, particularly adopted in Western science and philosophy
immanence: as opposed to transcendance, means existing or operating within, inherent (Deluze)
the rhizome: "Deluze, A Thousand Plateaus (1987), defines a rhizome as a decentralized, non-hierarchical, and multi-directional network (like ginger or mycelium) where any point can connect to any other point. It contrasts with "arborescent" or tree-like structures that rely on a single root, center, or linear hierarchy." rhizomatic intermediality: combines Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari’s concept of the rhizome with intermediality (the interplay, crossing, or synthesis between different artistic or communication media like text, music, visual art, and digital platforms)


Artists Minds are Full Of References

Bernstein interprets this quote as artists minds being so saturated with other work that originality becomes difficult. Intermediality is not just the crossing of boundaries, but is defined in this essay as a mutation or violent birth of a new medium all together, a definition supported by the 1977 book Image — Music — Text by Roland Barthes. Intermediality is described as "between the between", as Dick Higgins stated in 1966 intermedium, “intermediality has always been a possibility since the most ancient times . . . it remains a possibility wherever the desire to fuse two or more existing media exists” (25)

“Our debt to tradition through reading and conversation is so massive, our protest so rare and insignificant — and this commonly on the ground of other reading or hearing... “in large sense, one would say there is no pure originality. All minds quote. Old and new make the warp and woof of every moment. There is no thread that is not a twist of these two strands” Ralph Waldo Emerson, Essay: Quotation and Originality (543)

rhizomatic intermedia[lity] is the quasi-ontological plane underlying all media, out of which the specific media that we know percolate, so to speak. Then there is also an epistemological side to it: we can only refer to media by using other media (the default example being language as a primary medium). So, in a way there is one intermedia[lity] that comes first, which is the quicksand out of which specific media emerge, and a second intermedia[lity] that focuses on the various interconnections possible

First there is the media itself, the actual things to reference, which in themselves can be mutations or references.. then following this there is intermediality, the shaping of meaning by other texts, contexts, etc... this cycle has always been and will always be.

Writing Essays About Intermediality and Its Diversity is Meta...

Terms:

synthetic intermediality: a fusion of different media to super-media (or a Gesamtkunstwer, a Wagnerian concept)
formal (or transmedial) intermediality: " the concept that there are formal structures (such as narrative structures) that are not specific to one me- 4 dium but can be found (perhaps differently instantiated) in different media.. this model uses the concept of transmedial devices, and has the problem that “media specificity” cannot be conceptualized within it"
transformational intermediality: "the representation of one medium through another medium (recently the term “re- mediation” has been suggested for this). Here we must question whether this fits “intermediality” at all, because a represented medium is not longer a medium but a representation"
ontological intermediality: the view that media do not exist in isolation, but are inherently constituted by and through their relations with other media within a broader network or ecology.

Jens Schröter outlines in his chapter, “Four Models of Intermediality,” the field and discourse of “intermediality” is very diverse.

This essay is itself a meta-theoretical approach to defining the topic, as the essay heavily is dependant on its referneces, arguing that combindations of these references can form different models of intermediality

Analog vs Digital Photography

Alfred Eisenstaedt's 1945 photograph V-J Day in Times Square

Bernstein argues that technology being adopted in the digital photography process does not necissarily make the end product more or less truthful, authentic, or real. The photograph is a representation of a moment in time, but the technology used to capture it does not change the meaning of the image. He cites W. J. T. Mitchell’s essay on “Realism and the Digital Image”, which aslo aims to undercut the prevailing myth that digital photography has a different ontology than chemical-based photography.
